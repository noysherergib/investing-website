The building blocks

Common types of investments

Higher possible returns usually come with bigger ups and downs.

📈

Stocks

Small pieces of ownership in companies. They have the most growth potential, but prices can swing a lot from year to year.

Risk
🧺

Index funds & ETFs

One purchase buys a whole basket of stocks or bonds, such as the 500 largest US companies. They're diversified and usually cheap.

Risk
🏠

Real estate

Property, owned directly or through REITs. It can pay rental income and grow in value, but buying or selling it directly is slow.

Risk
📜

Bonds

Loans to governments or companies that pay you interest. They're steadier than stocks, but long-term growth is usually lower.

Risk
💵

Cash & savings

High-yield savings and money market funds. Very safe and easy to access, but inflation can slowly eat away at it.

Risk

Good to know

Frequently asked questions

How much money do I need to start?

Very little. Many brokers have no minimums and let you buy fractional shares, so you can start with $10. Building the habit matters more than the amount.

What is diversification?

Spreading money across many investments so one bad performer can't sink you. An index fund that holds hundreds of companies is an easy way to diversify.

Should I try to time the market?

Research consistently shows that even professionals struggle to predict short-term moves. Investing a steady amount on a schedule (dollar-cost averaging) removes the guesswork.

Why do fees matter so much?

A 1% yearly fee sounds tiny, but over 30 years it can take a large share of your final balance, because the money lost to fees never gets to compound. Low-cost funds help.

What should I do before investing?

A common order is: build an emergency fund of a few months of expenses, pay off high-interest debt, take any employer retirement match, and then invest more.
